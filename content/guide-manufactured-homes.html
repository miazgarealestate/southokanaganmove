Sources checked 12 November 2026. General guidance, not legal, lending or insurance advice. Rules and annual limits change; confirm with the named authority.

1. The label and the registry

  • In BC, a used manufactured home may only be offered for sale if it carries an approval mark (such as a CSA label) or a Technical Safety BC Silver Label. See Technical Safety BC.
  • If the CSA label has been removed or lost, the home must be re-certified: a licensed electrical contractor inspects the wiring and an electrical approval label is issued. See BC Manufactured Home Registry.
  • These labels relate to the electrical components only. Registration with the Manufactured Home Registry is a separate matter. See BCFSA: manufactured homes information.
  • Unpermitted electrical alterations can invalidate an existing label, according to the BC Real Estate Association. Ask for permits for any wiring or gas work. See BCREA.

2. The land

SituationWhat it means
Home on land you ownYou own both. Lenders and insurers still look at the home's age, label and condition.
Home in a park, rented padYou own the home and rent the pad. Your tenancy is governed by the Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act and your tenancy agreement.
Home on leased reserve landYou hold a lease, not title. Financing, resale and consent rules depend on the lease, so have a lawyer review it before you pay a deposit.

3. Pad rent and park tenancy

  • A park landlord may raise pad rent once in 12 months, with at least three months' notice, and not above the annual limit. See BC: rent increases for manufactured homes.
  • The provincial general limit is 2.3% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027. For manufactured home parks the regulation also allows a proportional amount for increases in local government levies and regulated utility fees. See BC news release on the 2027 limit and form RTB-45.
  • Ask for the park rules in writing before you offer: age limits, pets, rentals, who may buy, and what the pad rent includes.

4. Before you remove subjects

  • Confirm the approval label and registry details in person, not only in the listing.
  • Ask your lender and insurer, by name, whether they will cover this home on this land, with its year and label.
  • Have the home inspected, including electrical, plumbing, roof and skirting, and the pad or foundation. For homes with wells or septic, see the rural inspection guide.
  • If it is in a park, ask whether the park is for sale or has announced redevelopment, and read the tenancy agreement.
