Living in Osoyoos

A lakeside town at the south end of the Okanagan, with a strong seasonal rhythm and a mix of housing types.

The short version

Osoyoos is a small town on Osoyoos Lake, next to the U.S. border. It has a lake-and-resort character, a busy summer, and a quieter off-season. It suits people who want walkable access to the water and town services, and who are comfortable with seasonal change.

Setting and climate

The Town of Osoyoos describes a hot, dry, sunny climate in the rain shadow of the Cascades. For actual temperature and precipitation, use Environment Canada's climate normals. Summer heat and smoke are both part of life here; see Moving to the South Okanagan.

Housing you will see

  • Single-family homes in town and nearby subdivisions.
  • Townhomes and condominiums, many strata-titled, some with short-term rental rules. Read the bylaws.
  • Manufactured homes, including in parks.
  • Lakefront and near-lake property, which has its own considerations: shoreline rules, docks, flood and erosion questions, and insurance.
  • Rural and acreage property outside town limits, which falls under RDOS rules rather than the Town's. See Buying rural property.

Which government covers which property

Inside the Town boundary, the Town of Osoyoos handles zoning, building permits and most local services. Outside it, the Regional District of Okanagan-Similkameen (RDOS) handles land use for areas such as Electoral Area A, which includes rural Osoyoos and Anarchist Mountain. Osoyoos Indian Band reserve lands are also nearby, and tenure on reserve land is different from ordinary freehold. Confirm which jurisdiction applies before you assume anything about permitted use.

Short-term rentals and recreational use

Rules for short-term rentals vary by municipality, by zone and by strata. Do not rely on a listing that says a property can be rented short-term. Check the current bylaw with the Town or RDOS and, for strata, the strata's own bylaws and rules.

Things to check before you buy in Osoyoos

  • Zoning and permitted uses with the Town or RDOS, whichever applies.
  • Strata documents, if applicable: minutes, financial statements, depreciation report, bylaws, special levies.
  • Insurance quotes before removing subjects, especially for lakefront and hillside property.
  • Whether you are on municipal water and sewer or on a well and septic.
  • Noise and traffic at the times of year you will actually be there.

Visiting

Come in summer and again in a quieter month. The town feels very different in each.
