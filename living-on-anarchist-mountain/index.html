Living on Anarchist Mountain

A rural residential area above Osoyoos, where the property itself, not just the house, needs careful checking.

The short version

Anarchist Mountain is the rural area on the hillside above Osoyoos, east of town along Highway 3. It falls under RDOS Electoral Area A and is served by the Anarchist Mountain Volunteer Fire Department. Properties are generally rural residential or acreage rather than town lots, so the questions are different from a house in town.

From Pat's experience

Pat moved his family from the Lower Mainland to Anarchist Mountain about three years ago. His background in construction and renovation is why he looks closely at how a property is built and serviced, not just how it looks. This page separates that personal experience from the verified facts below.

Verified: how the area is governed and served

  • Land use outside the Town of Osoyoos is handled by the RDOS. Check zoning, permitted uses, secondary dwellings and building rules with the RDOS before you plan anything.
  • Fire protection comes from a volunteer fire department listed on the RDOS Fire Services page. Ask your insurer how fire-protection classification affects your quote.

What to check on any property up here

  • Water: Is it a private well, a shared system or another source? Ask for well records, flow and quality testing, and find out who owns and maintains any shared system.
  • Wastewater: Septic systems should have records and a recent inspection by a qualified person. See Buying rural property.
  • Access: Is the road public or private? Who plows and maintains it? Is there a road-use agreement?
  • Wildfire: Ask insurers early. Defensible-space work around the home affects both safety and insurability. See FireSmart BC.
  • Utilities and internet: Confirm the power provider and what internet and cell service are actually available at that address.
  • Winter: Elevation can mean different conditions from the valley floor. Visit in winter if you can.

Is it right for you?

It suits people who want space, views and quiet, and who are comfortable maintaining their own water, septic and driveway. It is less suited to people who want to walk to shops or rely on public services. Many people who move here from the city underestimate how much more there is to maintain.
