Buying Rural Property in BC

The checks that matter most when your water, wastewater, road and fire risk are your own responsibility.

This is general guidance, not legal, engineering or insurance advice. Each item below should be confirmed with the right professional or authority for the specific property.

Water

  • Find out whether the property is on a private well, a shared well, a community system or another source.
  • Ask for well records, drilling logs, flow rate and recent water-quality test results. Consider testing for yourself.
  • Since the Water Sustainability Act came into force, groundwater use beyond household purposes requires a licence. Ask the Province how this applies to any irrigation or other non-domestic use you plan. See Province of BC, groundwater and wells.
  • If the water comes from a shared system, ask who owns it, who maintains it and what it costs.

Septic and wastewater

  • Ask for permits, as-built drawings, maintenance and pumping records.
  • Have the system inspected by a qualified person. A home inspection does not normally assess a septic system in depth.
  • Replacement can be expensive, so treat the inspection result as part of the price discussion.

Road access

  • Confirm whether the road is public or private and who plows and maintains it.
  • Check for registered right-of-way, easement or road-use agreements on the title.
  • Test the access in bad weather if you can.

Zoning, permits and the ALR

  • Confirm zoning and permitted uses with the municipality or the RDOS. Do not assume you can add a suite, a shop or a second dwelling.
  • Check whether the property is in the Agricultural Land Reserve with the Agricultural Land Commission. ALR status limits subdivision and non-farm use.
  • Ask for permits and final inspections on any additions, outbuildings or finished basements. Unpermitted work can affect insurance, resale and your obligations.

Title and legal

  • Order a current title search from the Land Title and Survey Authority and read the charges, covenants and right-of-way documents.
  • Consider a survey or a surveyor's sketch if boundaries or access are unclear.
  • Use a lawyer or notary for the conveyance.

Wildfire and insurance

  • Get insurance quotes before you remove your subjects. Availability and cost can vary with location, fire-protection services and vegetation around the home.
  • Learn what defensible-space work would be expected. FireSmart BC has practical guidance.
  • Check BC Wildfire Service resources for current conditions.

Building and systems

  • Hire a qualified home inspector, and add specialists where needed: roof, heating, electrical, foundation.
  • Ask about heating source, fuel storage and any wood-stove certification.
  • Pat's construction and renovation background is one reason he helps buyers look past finishes to how a home is built and maintained.

Costs people forget

  • Property Transfer Tax and other closing costs. Rates and exemptions change, so check the current Province of BC page.
  • Equipment for acreage, such as a tractor or pump, and ongoing maintenance.
  • Higher insurance, if applicable, and longer distances to services.
